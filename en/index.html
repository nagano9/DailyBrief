GPT-6 Astra becomes first deployed model to reach Critical cybersecurity level under OpenAI Preparedness Framework

What changed
OpenAI published the safety overview for GPT-6 Astra confirming it is the first model in broad deployment to reach the Critical tier of cybersecurity capability under the Preparedness Framework, meaning the model can provide meaningful uplift to actors seeking to conduct cyberattacks at a level previously designated as a deployment-blocking threshold. [3]
Why it matters
This continues the enterprise-ai-data-privacy-contracts thread and marks a structural escalation: OpenAI has deployed a model it previously classified as requiring extraordinary mitigations before release. The framework's Critical designation was designed as a near-prohibitive bar; its breach signals that the bar is being managed through mitigations rather than withheld deployment, shifting the liability and governance question from the lab to enterprise adopters and regulators.
Second-order effect
Enterprises that have accepted GPT-6 Astra into production workflows under standard commercial agreements now carry indirect exposure to a model with confirmed offensive cyber capability. Insurers, legal counsel, and procurement officers have not yet repriced this exposure, because the designation appeared in a safety document rather than a commercial disclosure. Expect contract renegotiations and new exclusion clauses once legal teams absorb the Preparedness Framework language.
Action
Pull the GPT-6 Astra safety overview and map its Critical-tier mitigations against your organization's existing AI acceptable-use policy. Identify any production workflows where the model has write access to external systems or code repositories, and flag those for legal review before the next contract renewal cycle.
