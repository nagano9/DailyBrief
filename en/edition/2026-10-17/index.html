New PLN chief Suroso Isnandar inherits 700-plus stalled generation projects and a structurally weak transmission network

Energy & Power Material Structural · 30x since 2026-09-17
What changed
Suroso Isnandar was officially appointed Direktur Utama of PT PLN (Persero) on 14 October 2026, replacing Darmawan Prasodjo. IESR, Bisnis.com, and Bloomberg Technoz each published assessments the following day noting that more than 700 generation projects remain unexecuted and that transmission investment is the primary bottleneck preventing affordable electricity delivery. [2][6][7][11]
Why it matters
This continues the pln-grid-investment thread, now at structural depth after 29 appearances in 30 days. The new leadership transition is the moment at which agenda-setting becomes possible, but the inherited backlog means the default outcome is continuation of the same constraints: generation capacity queued but unable to dispatch because transmission capacity is insufficient. IESR explicitly states the new director must pursue business transformation, not merely program continuity.
Second-order effect
A leadership change without a parallel regulatory or financing instrument for transmission will not clear the backlog. The 700-plus project queue represents committed but undelivered capacity; if transmission bottlenecks persist, the capital already deployed in generation earns no return and tariff pressure on PLN intensifies. The institutional risk is that Suroso's mandate gets consumed by near-term operational fires while the structural transmission deficit compounds, making the 2045 clean-energy target arithmetically harder each quarter.
Action
Map the 700-plus projects by interconnection readiness: identify which subset is blocked by transmission availability versus permitting versus financing. That segmentation determines where Suroso's first 90 days should concentrate and which projects are candidates for the transmission subholding structure IEEFA has proposed.

2

Zero-percent income tax on BUMN restructuring remains inoperative as two implementing regulations are still missing

Corporate & SOE Material Structural · 30x since 2026-09-17
What changed
The Directorate General of Taxes (DJP) confirmed publicly on 15 October 2026 that the PPh final 0% incentive for BUMN restructuring transactions cannot be applied because both the Government Regulation (PP) and the Finance Ministry Regulation (PMK) needed to operationalize the measure have not been issued. Multiple outlets including CNBC Indonesia, Bloomberg Technoz, ANTARA, and Kompas.com corroborate this regulatory gap. [3][8][12][13]
Why it matters
This continues the danantara-bumn-karya-restructuring thread at structural depth. The zero-percent tax incentive was announced as a key enabler of the Danantara-led consolidation of SOEs, including BUMN Karya. Without the PP and PMK, every restructuring transaction that relies on asset transfers, mergers, or equity conversions faces standard corporate income tax rates, making the economics of consolidation materially worse and slowing the entire reform timetable.
Second-order effect
The regulatory gap creates an adverse selection problem: SOEs with weaker balance sheets, which most need restructuring, are also the ones least able to absorb full tax costs on intercompany transfers. If Danantara proceeds with consolidations before the regulations are issued, it either absorbs the tax cost at the holding level or delays transactions, both of which compress the capital available for strategic reinvestment. The longer the gap persists, the more restructuring momentum is lost to administrative uncertainty.
Action
Check whether any BUMN restructuring transaction scheduled for Q4 2026 has a closing condition tied to the PPh 0% rule. If so, assess whether the PP and PMK can realistically be issued before year-end or whether the transaction timeline needs to be renegotiated.

3

BSI schedules Extraordinary General Meeting for Rp 3.4 trillion rights issue to meet 15% free-float requirement

Corporate & SOE Decision-forcing Structural · 30x since 2026-09-17
What changed
Bank Syariah Indonesia (BRIS) announced it will hold an Extraordinary General Meeting (RUPSLB) to approve a rights issue of approximately 6.8 billion shares targeting Rp 3.4 trillion in proceeds. The stated purposes are to meet the OJK-mandated 15% free-float threshold and to strengthen sharia financing capacity. Multiple sources including CNBC Indonesia, Kontan, and Bloomberg Technoz corroborate the announcement. [5][10][14]
Why it matters
This continues the danantara-bumn-karya-restructuring thread. BSI's rights issue is a concrete capital market execution within the Danantara SOE portfolio, testing whether state-controlled banks can access equity markets at scale without Danantara absorbing the dilution or guaranteeing the offer. The free-float compliance deadline imposed by OJK is non-negotiable, making this a forced transaction rather than an opportunistic one.
Second-order effect
If BSI's rights issue is undersubscribed, the government or Danantara-linked entities will need to take up the unsubscribed portion, concentrating ownership further and reducing the free-float that the exercise was designed to increase. That scenario would simultaneously strain Danantara's capital deployment capacity and signal to markets that SOE equity issuances require implicit state backstops, raising the implied cost of future SOE capital raises.
Action
Assess the rights issue pricing against BSI's current market capitalization and the implied dilution for existing minority shareholders. Determine whether the RUPSLB date leaves sufficient time for institutional pre-marketing before the OJK free-float deadline.

4

Anthropic privately advances its own AI safety bill to U.S. lawmakers, shifting governance from reactive to industry-drafted

AI & Frontier Models Emerging Structural · 29x since 2026-09-17
What changed
Anthropic has quietly pitched a draft AI safety bill directly to U.S. lawmakers, according to reporting by Politico as cited by Reuters and Bloomberg. The bill is Anthropic's own formulation, not a response to a legislative proposal, meaning a frontier AI lab is now proactively authoring the regulatory standard it will be measured against. [4][9]
Why it matters
This continues the ai-model-safety-frontier thread. The move changes the governance dynamic: rather than labs lobbying against external proposals, Anthropic is attempting to set the definitional terms of what constitutes safe AI development. If adopted even partially, Anthropic's framing of safety standards, evaluation thresholds, and liability allocation will become the baseline against which competitors including OpenAI and Google DeepMind are judged.
Second-order effect
Sovereign AI procurement teams and enterprise legal departments that reference U.S. federal AI safety standards in contracts or procurement criteria will find those standards potentially authored by one of the vendors they are evaluating. That conflict of interest is not hypothetical: if Anthropic's bill defines safety benchmarks that Anthropic's own models pass by construction, the standard ceases to be independent. Procurement frameworks that treat U.S. regulatory approval as a sufficient safety signal will need to add independent evaluation layers.
Action
Review any AI procurement or partnership agreements that reference U.S. federal AI safety certification or regulatory compliance as a selection criterion. Add a clause requiring independent third-party evaluation regardless of regulatory status, given that the regulatory standard itself may be vendor-influenced.

U.S. ITC finalizes antidumping and countervailing duties on solar imports from Indonesia, India, and Laos

Energy & Power Material Structural · 9x since 2026-09-17
What changed
The U.S. International Trade Commission issued its final affirmative determination on antidumping and countervailing duties against solar module imports from Indonesia, India, and Laos. The final vote follows earlier affirmative dumping and subsidy findings by the U.S. Department of Commerce, and secures the issuance of federal duty orders, closing the U.S. market to these origins at competitive price points. [1]
Why it matters
Indonesian solar manufacturers that had established or were targeting U.S. export routes now face a permanent tariff barrier. The decision coincides with China's proposed ban on exports of perovskite and tandem solar manufacturing technology, which would simultaneously restrict Indonesian producers' access to next-generation process technology. The two developments together compress both the export market and the technology upgrade path for Indonesia's solar manufacturing sector.
Second-order effect
Indonesian solar panel capacity that was oriented toward U.S. exports will need reorientation toward domestic demand or alternative export markets such as the EU or ASEAN. This accelerates the case for PLN and Danantara-linked offtakers to sign long-term domestic procurement agreements with Indonesian manufacturers, potentially at prices above current spot to keep domestic capacity solvent. Without that demand signal, Indonesian module producers may exit or consolidate, reducing the domestic supply chain that the government's renewable energy targets depend on.
Action
Identify which Indonesian solar manufacturers had material U.S. export exposure and assess their financial position under the new duty regime. Determine whether PLN's procurement pipeline for utility-scale solar can absorb redirected domestic supply, and whether a government-backed offtake mechanism would be needed to prevent capacity closures.
